## Stability Constraints (The "Bodyguard")

`OrbitalConfig::validate()` in `kelvin-kdf/src/config.rs` (lines 253–287)
rejects configurations that fail three checks:

### 1. Identical Positions

Any two bodies with `position.length_squared() == 0`.

**Impact:** Negligible. With each body's position drawn from a continuous-like
distribution over a sphere of radius 50–250 AU, and the sun jittered by ~2^-44 AU,
the probability of exact collision is ≈ 2^-128 × 2^-128 × C(5,2) ≈ 2^-254.

### 2. Initial Collapse (Too Close)

Any pair closer than `MIN_SEPARATION = Fixed::from_raw(1 << 40)` ≈ 6×10^-8 AU
(≈ 9 km).

**Impact:** Near-zero for planets orbiting at 100+ AU, whose distance is always
> 1 AU from each other (≫ 9 km). Rejection rate < 10^-12.

### 3. Initial Ejection (Unbound Energy)

A body with total specific energy ≥ `EJECTION_ENERGY_THRESHOLD = 2^20` (raw).

**Analysis:** For a planet at radius ~100 AU with circular velocity ≈ 0.63 AU/yr:

```
KE ≈ 2.14 × 10^8  (raw fixed-point)
PE ≈ -4.22 × 10^8  (raw fixed-point)
Total = -2.08 × 10^8  < 2^20 = 1,048,576
```

Ejection only occurs when velocity is >> escape velocity. With the generation
code using `v_mag = 1/sqrt(radius) × 6.3` (circular orbit approximation), the
vast majority of configurations are bound.

**Rejection rate estimate:** < 10^-3 (only extreme random velocity directions
near escape).

A body's total specific energy becomes ≥ `EJECTION_ENERGY_THRESHOLD` at

pump energy into a body above escape velocity.

**Impact on keyspace:** The configuration is rejected if *any* check during
the full simulation fails. This means the accept/reject decision depends on
the entire chaotic trajectory, not just initial conditions.

The rejection rate depends on the specific initial conditions generated. For

**Estimated mid-simulation rejection rate:** < 10^-6 per configuration.

#### 5. Mid-Simulation Collapse

well-spaced initial orbits.

**Estimated mid-simulation rejection rate:** < 10^-4 per configuration.

## Physical Binding (Hardening)

As of the latest hardening phase, the entropy extractor (SHA3-512) no longer hashes only the orbital positions. The resulting seed is now cryptographically bound to the "Physical Reality" of the simulation.

### 1. Constant Binding
Even if $G$, `softening`, and `dt` are constant for a specific security level, they are explicitly fed into the hash chain. This ensures that a configuration is only valid for the physical laws it was generated under.

### 2. Instantaneous Force Vectors
The hash chain now includes the **acceleration vectors** acting on every body at each reseed interval.
- **Entropy**: While derived from positions and masses, these vectors capture the "instability" of the system.
- **Security**: This prevents "phantom" configurations where a body is placed in a way that bypasses the physics engine, as the force vectors would immediately diverge from expected values.

### 3. Step Binding
The `step` counter is hashed, ensuring that the same physical state at two different points in time produces distinct entropy seeds.


`rand::thread_rng()` uses ChaCha12 with a 256-bit seed (on standard Rust).

While the expression space is 2^1113, the **observable** keyspace is capped by
the RNG seed entropy: **2^256 distinct seeds**.

This is equivalent to the 256-bit security level of AES-256 — adequate for all
practical purposes. The 2^1113 expression space provides headroom for structural
diversity even if the underlying RNG were weakened.

In practice this means there are at most **2^256 distinct, non-overlapping JSON
configurations** that can be generated — one per unique RNG seed.

### Practical Encryption Limit (Key Schedule Exhaustion)

While the keyspace is astronomically large, the **practical encryption capacity** of a single `Kelvin` instance is limited by the key schedule's virtual step budget:

```
max_keys = safe_steps / reseed_interval
```

For a standard-level configuration:
- `safe_steps` ≈ 73 (from Lyapunov estimation)
- `reseed_interval` = 10
- `max_keys` = 7

Each key provides ~4 GiB of safe encryption (conservative estimate). So a single
`Kelvin` instance can encrypt at most **~28 GiB** before the key schedule is
exhausted. Beyond this, a new configuration must be generated.

This is not a keyspace limitation — it is a **forward secrecy** safeguard. The
Lyapunov horizon ensures that even if an attacker compromises the current key,
they cannot derive past or future keys beyond the chaotic regime's predictability
horizon.
